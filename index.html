ENIAC

1945

The First Electronic Computer

ENIAC (Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer) was the first programmable, electronic, general-purpose digital computer. It was Turing-complete and could solve a large class of numerical problems through reprogramming.

Weight: 30 tons
Vacuum Tubes: 17,468
Power: 150 kW
Speed: 100 kHz

Internet Era (1990s)

Tim Berners-Lee

Inventor of the World Wide Web

1955 - present

The Story Behind the Web

In 1989, while working at CERN, Tim Berners-Lee proposed a system to manage information using "hypertext" - a way to link documents across different computers. His vision was to create a universal information space where anyone could share and access knowledge.

The first website was built at CERN and went live on August 6, 1991. It explained what the World Wide Web was and how to create your own web pages.

🌐 First Website

info.cern.ch - Still online today!

💡 The Idea

Berners-Lee was frustrated with information loss at CERN. Scientists from around the world needed a better way to share data. His solution combined:

  • HTML (HyperText Markup Language)
  • HTTP (HyperText Transfer Protocol)
  • URLs (Uniform Resource Locators)
  • The first web browser

How It Changed the World

The Web grew exponentially:

1991: 1 website
1994: ~3,000 websites
2000: 17 million websites
Today: 1.8+ billion websites

Mobile & Cloud (2000s-2010s)

📱 ☁️

iPhone

2007

The Device That Changed Everything

On January 9, 2007, Steve Jobs introduced the iPhone, describing it as "an iPod, a phone, and an internet communicator." It revolutionized the mobile industry with its multi-touch interface and lack of physical keyboard.

The App Store launched in 2008, creating an entirely new economy and changing how we use smartphones forever.

☁️ Cloud Computing

The cloud allowed smartphones to become truly powerful:

  • iCloud (2011) - Sync across devices
  • Google Drive - Cloud storage
  • Dropbox - File sharing
  • AWS - Powering the internet

Intelligent Machine

The Rise of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence aims to create machines that can think, learn, and adapt like humans. The concept dates back to ancient times, but modern AI began in the 1950s with Alan Turing's question: "Can machines think?"

🎯 Purpose & Goals

  • Automate repetitive tasks
  • Process vast amounts of data
  • Make predictions and decisions
  • Understand human language
  • Recognize patterns and images
  • Solve complex problems
